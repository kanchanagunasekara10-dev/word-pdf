      How it works

      1. Pick your files. Drop one or a hundred .docx documents onto the page.
      2. Choose your page setup. Size, orientation and margins, applied to every file in the batch.
      3. Convert. Each document is read with mammoth.js, laid out as HTML, then printed to PDF with html2pdf.js.
      4. Download. One PDF, or the whole batch as a ZIP.

      Why this is private

      Most online converters upload your document to a server, convert it there, and hand you a link. SwiftPDF has no server. The conversion code runs in your browser, your file is read from disk into memory, and the finished PDF is written straight back to your downloads folder. Nothing is transmitted, logged, or stored — you can watch the network tab and confirm it, or disconnect from the internet after the page loads and it will still work.

      FAQ

      Can I convert old .doc files?
      No — only .docx. Open the file in Word and use Save As → .docx first.
      How many files can I convert at once?
      There is no fixed limit. They are converted one at a time so your browser stays responsive; a large batch simply takes longer.
      Will the PDF look exactly like Word?
      Close, but not pixel-identical. Headings, bold and italic text, lists, tables, links and images all carry over. Word's headers, footers and page numbers do not.
      Can I select text in the resulting PDF?
      No. Each page is rendered as a high-resolution image, which is what keeps the layout faithful. If you need selectable text, a server-side converter such as LibreOffice is the better tool.
      Is there a file size limit?
      Files up to 25 MB each. The real limit is your device's memory.
